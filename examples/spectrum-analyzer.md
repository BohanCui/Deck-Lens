# Example: Spectrum Analyzer Software Enhancement — Pitch Lens

A worked example of the **Pitch lens** (10-beat structure) applied to a competition pitch for a spectrum analyzer software secondary-development project.

---

## Situation
- **Audience:** Competition judges (non-technical)
- **Context:** Competition pitch
- **Goal:** Win — convince judges the project is innovative, practical, and viable
- **Lens:** Pitch (confirmed)
- **Output:** PPTX + speech script
- **Design template:** `design-default.md` (Royal Blue, bright background)

---

## 10-Beat Breakdown

### Beat 1 — Hook
> In recent years, radio spectrum resources have grown increasingly scarce, and competitions and monitoring tasks rely more and more heavily on spectrum analysis equipment. Using a spectrum analyzer efficiently and accurately has become a core skill for frontline operators.

One slide. White background, navy headline, royal blue accent. Maybe a trend graphic.

### Beat 2 — Theme & Slogan
> **Spectrum monitoring, one step faster.**

One slide. Royal blue accent block, large headline on white. Minimal.

### Beat 3 — Pain Points
Concrete, relatable pain with the stock device:
- **Far too few markers** — the stock unit supports only 6, nowhere near enough for complex tasks
- **Markers stuck at the top** — they only show at the top of the screen, impossible to locate directly on the spectrum
- **Slow lookup** — every signal found needs extra time to cross-check its position
- **Easy to lose track** — with many markers, operators forget which were checked
- **Transient signals missed** — moving fast without watching the screen means short-lived signals slip by

One to two slides. Icons + short phrases. Make the pain feel real.

### Beat 4 — Status Quo Gap
> - Large manufacturers: mature product lines, **unwilling to customize for small-volume users**
> - Industry-wide: **no one has done secondary software development based on real usage needs**
> - Conclusion: a clear market gap — and we chose to fill it

One slide. Three-column layout. Gold accent on the "us" column.

### Beat 5 — Our Approach
Feature by feature, fundamental → impressive:
1. **Marker count breakthrough** — far beyond the stock 6-marker limit, meeting complex monitoring needs
2. **Type-based grouping** — markers grouped by signal type, no more confusion
3. **Dead-simple operation** — one-tap add, delete, jump; zero learning curve
4. **One-tap photo import** — photograph the device screen and markers are auto-recognized and imported
5. **Automatic monitoring & alerts** — set a threshold and the system detects anomalies and warns you, hands-free

Two to three slides. One feature per visual block.

### Beat 6 — Visual Demo
Real screenshots / demo frames:
- Before: stock interface (6 markers, cramped, top strip)
- After: enhanced interface (many markers, grouped, annotated directly on the spectrum)
- After: one-tap photo import flow
- After: automatic monitoring alert screen

Let the visuals talk. Minimal text overlay.

### Beat 7 — Summary Map
One slide. Pain point → solution mapping:

| Pain point | Our solution |
|------------|--------------|
| Too few markers | Marker limit removed, add as needed |
| Can't locate on spectrum | Markers shown directly on the spectrum |
| Slow lookup | Grouped management, one-tap jump |
| Lost track of progress | Grouped labels, clear status |
| Transient signals missed | Automatic monitoring, real-time alerts |

Navy header row, white body. Gold on the header.

### Beat 8 — Pricing & Authorization
> **What's included, and how it's licensed.** *(Competition context — pricing is illustrative.)*
> - **Base license** — full marker system, grouping, one-tap operation
> - **Pro license** — adds one-tap photo import + automatic monitoring & alerts
> - **Per-device authorization** — license bound to device; updates included for 12 months
> - Clear, one-page terms — no hidden costs

One to two slides. If the competition doesn't expect pricing, frame this as a value/deliverables slide instead — but keep the clarity.

### Beat 9 — Future Value
> This is just the beginning. Reserved expansion space:
> - Support for more spectrum analyzer models
> - Cloud sync for multi-operator collaborative monitoring
> - AI-assisted signal recognition (planned)
> - Open API for integration with existing monitoring platforms

One slide. Forward-looking, confident, no overpromising.

### Beat 10 — Closing Slogan + CTA
> **Spectrum monitoring, one step faster.**
>
> **Our ask:** [adoption decision / pilot approval / further evaluation] — we'd like to move to a hands-on trial with your team.

Echo Beat 2 exactly, same visual design. Then one clear, specific call to action — not inspiration alone.

---

## Tone Notes
- Confident, not exaggerated
- Pain points should resonate with judges, not just complain about the stock manufacturer
- Features framed as "what the operator can do," not "what we built"
- Pricing slide: direct and honest; flag clearly that pricing is illustrative in a competition
- Closing CTA: one specific ask, delivered with the same energy as the opening slogan
