# Mode: Pitch
**Use for:** Competition, fundraising, external client demo, product launch

Audience is external. They don't know you. You need to earn their attention, make them feel the problem, show your solution, and close with a clear ask.

---

## The 10-Beat Pitch Structure

| #   | Beat                        | Purpose                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| --- | --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1   | **Hook**                    | Open with a trend, stat, or observation that earns attention. ("In recent years, X has become increasingly critical…")                                                                                                                                 |
| 2   | **Theme & Slogan**          | State your topic. Land a punchy tagline — ex: innovation, practicality, convenience. Set the emotional register for the whole deck.                                                                                                                    |
| 3   | **Pain Points**             | Make the audience feel the problem viscerally. Be specific. Use real numbers. Paint the before-picture.                                                                                                                                                |
| 4   | **Status Quo Gap**          | Show that existing approaches fall short. Frame to context: competitor products, industry-wide practices, or incumbent solutions. If Beat 1 didn't fully establish the backdrop, add industry context here. Position yourself as the clear gap-filler. |
| 5   | **Our Approach**            | Introduce your solution feature by feature. Order: most fundamental → most impressive. Lead with what the user gains, not how it was built.                                                                                                            |
| 6   | **Visual Demo**             | Screenshots, photos, or demo footage. Real visuals only — no mockups unless clearly labeled. Show, don't just tell.                                                                                                                                    |
| 7   | **Summary Map**             | One slide. Map each pain point → your solution. The audience sees the complete before/after picture in one glance.                                                                                                                                     |
| 8   | **Pricing & Authorization** | What does this cost? What's included? What does the buyer/approver get access to? Be clear and specific. This slide earns trust — vagueness kills deals. Include: tiers (if any), what each covers, and any licensing or access terms.                 |
| 9   | **Future Value**            | What's the potential beyond today? What's reserved for future development? Signal vision and scalability without overpromising.                                                                                                                        |
| 10  | **Closing Slogan + CTA**    | Echo Beat 2 — same tagline, same energy. Then land a clear call to action: what do you want the audience to do next? (Approve, invest, sign up, follow up, schedule a call.) Never end on inspiration alone.                                           |

---

## Slide Count Guidance

| Beat | Slides |
|------|--------|
| 1 Hook | 1 |
| 2 Theme & Slogan | 1 |
| 3 Pain Points | 1–2 |
| 4 Status Quo Gap | 1 |
| 5 Our Approach | 2–3 |
| 6 Visual Demo | 2–4 |
| 7 Summary Map | 1 |
| 8 Pricing & Authorization | 1–2 |
| 9 Future Value | 1 |
| 10 Closing Slogan + CTA | 1 |
| **Total** | **~12–17 slides** |

---

## Tone Notes
- Confident but not arrogant
- Pain points should feel real — not just complaints about the incumbent
- Feature introductions: "what the user can do" not "what we built"
- Pricing slide: direct and honest. If it's a competition and pricing is hypothetical, frame it clearly.
- CTA: one specific ask, not multiple options
