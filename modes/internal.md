# Mode: Internal Report
**Use for:** Presenting to leadership, management, or cross-department stakeholders inside your organization

Audience already has context about the organization. They are busy. They want the conclusion first, then the reasoning. Do not make them sit through 10 minutes of buildup before you say what you want.

This mode follows the **Pyramid Principle**: lead with the answer, then support it.

---

## The 8-Beat Internal Structure

| # | Beat | Purpose |
|---|------|---------|
| 1 | **Executive Summary** | One slide. State the situation, your recommendation, and the expected outcome. The rest of the deck supports this — leadership should be able to stop here and already know what you're asking for. |
| 2 | **Background & Context** | Brief, neutral framing of why this topic is on the table. What triggered this review? What's the scope? |
| 3 | **Problem Statement** | Define the problem objectively. Avoid blame. Use data where available. What is the cost or impact of the current situation? |
| 4 | **Current Approach & Its Gaps** | How is this being handled today — by your team, another department, or an existing system? What specifically is falling short? Avoid making this a takedown; frame it as an honest assessment. |
| 5 | **Proposed Solution** | Your recommendation. What should change, and how? Lead with the outcome it produces, then describe the key components. Keep it high-level unless the audience needs detail. |
| 6 | **Evidence & Validation** | Why should they trust this will work? Pilot results, benchmarks, comparable examples inside or outside the org, expert input. This is where you earn credibility. |
| 7 | **Resource Requirements & Timeline** | What do you need to make this happen? Budget, headcount, tools, approvals, timeline. Be specific. Vague asks get vague answers. |
| 8 | **Next Steps & Ask** | What specific decision or action do you need from this audience, and by when? One clear ask. If there are options, present 2–3 with a recommended path. |

---

## Slide Count Guidance

| Beat | Slides |
|------|--------|
| 1 Executive Summary | 1 |
| 2 Background & Context | 1 |
| 3 Problem Statement | 1–2 |
| 4 Current Approach & Gaps | 1–2 |
| 5 Proposed Solution | 2–3 |
| 6 Evidence & Validation | 1–2 |
| 7 Resources & Timeline | 1–2 |
| 8 Next Steps & Ask | 1 |
| **Total** | **~9–14 slides** |

---

## Tone Notes
- Formal but direct — no slogans, no performance
- Start with the conclusion, not the journey
- Avoid "we worked really hard on this" framing — focus on outcomes and impact
- Be neutral about the current approach: make it a gap analysis, not a blame slide
- If you have appendix material (detailed data, org charts, full financials), put it after the main deck — don't clutter the flow
- The ask must be explicit: "We are requesting approval to proceed" / "We need a decision on X by [date]"
